# Parallel course development

## Shared design and implementation contract

Target a 1,450–1,600 m continuous course and representative clean 55–65 second laps, with three laps near three minutes. Measure first and flying laps separately; adjust meaningful route length and corner shapes rather than global speed or compulsory waiting. All figures here are tuning targets until measured.

Six physical sections introduce a major change roughly every ten seconds, with two or three smaller beats (turn reversal, sightline, item choice, crest, width change) inside each. No unchanged stretch should approach twenty seconds. Place recovery room after demanding sections. Keep one coherent landscape, one interactive hazard, one boost-dependent shortcut, and one competing normal racing line. Obstacles always leave a clear passing lane.

Use the current world-space driving, bounded ramp hops, drift boosts, item system and ordered checkpoints. No gaps, cannons, falling roads, true branching progress, or new vehicle mode. The shortcut is a continuous ground patch inside an expanded physical boundary, uses the same ground query and ordered progression, and has a clearly visible entry and exit. Boost overrides its off-road drag. AI initially stays on the main route.

Each course owns a descriptor and scenery entry point under `src/courses/<id>.js` and `src/courses/<id>-world.js`, with focused construction and animation helpers under `src/courses/<id>/`. Shared track construction translates authored control-point boundaries into actual arc-distance section ranges and caches route frames. Rendering, kart physics, shells, AI, pads and ramps consume that same metadata. A registry selects the course before constructing the game; changing course starts a fresh page/race so old world state cannot leak. Root-owned engine, registry and UI files are not edited by course agents.

Descriptor fields: `id`, `name`, `description`, `targetLength`, `controls` (world xyz), `sections` (six objects with `controlIndex`, `id`, `name`, `hint`, `halfWidth`, `material`, `color`, optional `grip`), `ramps` ({section,fraction,halfLength,height}), `pads` ({section,fraction,offset,duration}), `itemRows` ({section,fraction}), `shortcut` ({section,startFraction,endFraction,extraWidth}), `hazard` ({section,fraction,kind,label,parkOffset,minOffset,halfWidth,halfLength,safeLane,activation,period,warningSeconds}), `theme` ({sky,fog,ground,road,shoulder,hemisphere,ambientGround,sun,sunIntensity,exposure}), `buildWorld` (scenery function). Section widths are half-widths in metres; offsets are signed lateral world metres. Shortcut extends the positive/right boundary; design an inside-right curve there. Hazard occupies the right/central road and reserves the negative/left passing lane. The common hazard runtime handles the shared warning/movement/collision clock; themed meshes are owned by scenery.

### Units and validation

The registry validates every course descriptor when it loads, and `createTrack()` validates descriptors passed directly by tools or tests. Validation checks six sections, ordered in-range control indexes, finite xyz controls, positive widths and dimensions, section references, normalized fractions from 0 through 1, and required hazard and theme fields. Errors include the course ID and descriptor path.

Constructed road curves must have a horizontal radius of at least 40 metres (maximum curvature 0.025 / metre), including the lap seam. `createTrack()` measures adjacent route samples, exposes `minimumCurveRadius`, and rejects tighter bends. Round authored controls to satisfy this limit while preserving course length; do not rely on the kart braking to negotiate a pinched spline. This leaves regular racing lanes room above the kart's 22 m full-speed/boost turning radius. Drift turn radius varies with latched yaw and countersteering rather than using a fixed course-design value. See [handling tuning](../../docs/handling.md) for measurements and behavior.

`targetLength`, section `halfWidth`, ramp `halfLength` and `height`, pad and hazard offsets, hazard dimensions, and hazard activation/period values are metres (or seconds for time values). Control points are world-space metres in `[x, y, z]` order. `section`, `controlIndex`, and racer `x` are zero-based indexes or lane units as indicated; a lane unit converts to 6.25 lateral metres through `laneWidth()` and `laneFromOffset()`. Fractions are normalized within their referenced section. `grip` is a positive simulation tuning value rather than a distance or speed.

Scenery signature: `buildWorld(context)`, where context provides `THREE`, `scene`, `scenery`, `track`, `course`, `mats`, `textures`, `renderer`, `kit`, `hazardAt`. Shared ground, road, rails, markings, ramps, pads and pickups are engine-owned. Scenery builds place-specific props and returns `{update(time), animated: [groupsOrMeshes]}`. `kit` provides `material(color,extra)`, `mesh(geometry,material,parent,position,scale)`, `box(material,parent,position,scale)`, `groupAt(t,offset,parent)`, `sectorT(index,fraction)`, `asset(name,parent,position,scale)`, `batch(parent)`, `align(group,frame)`. Props must sit beyond the physical boundary or above a portal with at least 11 m camera clearance. Only the declared moving hazard occupies driveable road; decorative props have no separate collisions.

Validate six continuous sections, route length, route separation, widths, pad alignment, shortcut ground/progress, hazard warning and permanently clear lane, kart/shell edge agreement, finite projection, bounded hops, all five AI drivers finishing three laps, measured sector splits, desktop/mobile camera clearance, course switching and browser console. Keep geometry/material reuse and batch static meshes; avoid per-frame scenery allocation and large numbers of real-time lights. Document actual measurements and any remaining human-playtest tuning rather than claiming unmeasured performance.

## Review additions

Optional `surfaces: [{section,startFraction,endFraction,material,grip}]` overrides material/grip only within its shared arc range. Optional `elevated: [{section,startFraction,endFraction}]` marks supported road where terrain embankments must be omitted. New hazards use minOffset 0, parkOffset 12, halfWidth 1.35, halfLength 2.15, safeLane −5.5; camera overhead minimum 11 m, all prop footprints beyond actual edges. Shortcut must be on actual inside-right curve and rejoin before next tight corner. Root owns all shared files. Agents own only their `src/courses/<id>.js`, `src/courses/<id>-world.js`, proposal measurement notes, and `tests/<id>.test.js`.

`shortcut.drag` optionally scales unboosted off-road drag within the shortcut range (default 1); powder can use 1.25. Boost still overrides off-road drag. All roadside text signs and sector HUD labels are omitted, preserving the latest main-branch design; use flags, structural markers, beacon warnings and sightlines instead. World results declare `animated: [groupsOrMeshes]` for every dynamically referenced object so root can flatten/batch all other authored scenery. Finish overhead clears the camera at 13 m; posts sit beyond physical barriers.

## Living-world route extensions

A course may declare `verges: [{section,startFraction,endFraction,side,extraWidth,material,grip,drag}]`. Side is signed −1 / +1; extraWidth is metres. The shared track exposes `VERGES`, `vergeWidth(t,side)` and `vergeAt(t,offset)`. Patches taper over 12 metres or a third of their span. Positive expansion takes the maximum of the existing shortcut and new verge; negative verges open the left boundary. `surfaceAt` identifies the actual material, offroad grip and drag at the offset. Normal boosts override the rough-ground drag. All side routes retain the same ordered progress and continuous ground.

`kit.landGroup(t,offset,parent)` places upright scenery on the actual embankment. `kit.safeGroup(t,offset,footprint,parent)` rejects footprint overlaps against the nearest course arm. `kit.hasAsset(name)` identifies optional imported textured props; `kit.asset` supports both normalized Kenney mesh records and downloaded textured groups. Materials inherit downloaded normal/roughness maps through their shared color map.

# Turbo Trail — living worlds art direction

## Goal
Keep Turbo Trail's friendly, bright arcade style and turn all four courses into places racers want to explore. Aim for the visual richness and clear silhouettes associated with Wii / Wii U racing games: textured, rounded forms, warm directional sunlight, cool shade, layered landscapes, lively landmarks and expressive motion. Treat that as a visual target, not a claim of matching console hardware or Nintendo assets.

Each course needs a different spatial rhythm, signature landmark, distinctive foreground, animated environmental life, and driving decisions tied to its setting. Individual sectors should create changing experiences within that identity. Preserve the existing readable handling, modest jumps, race progression and approachable main line.

## Shared visual language
- Shapes: rounded, chunky, slightly exaggerated structures with actual construction detail. Give buildings foundations, trim, windows, doors and roof thickness. Give vegetation trunks, branches, irregular crowns and layered ground cover. Avoid repeated cones and spheres as the entire asset vocabulary.
- Materials: hand-painted color variation, believable texture scale and selective bump detail. Asphalt needs aggregate, wear and repaired seams; wood needs plank grain and fastening; rocks need strata and moss; grassy land needs broad color patches and small blades. Maintain recognizable material colors under lighting.
- Palette: honey sunlight and warm creams balanced by teal sky and cool forest shade. Sector accents are butter-yellow and coral in the festival, deep mint in the forest, pale ochre rock on the ridge, turquoise water by timber, terracotta and flour-cream in the mill, and peach blossom / apple-red in the orchard.
- Composition: foreground verge details, middle-distance landmarks, distant hills and atmospheric depth. Use purposeful clusters with open sightlines rather than uniform random scattering. Break the horizon with hills, buildings and treetops. Transition adjacent sectors with overlapping plants and materials.
- Light: directional sunlight with soft contact and cast shadows; subdued ambient fill; shade and mist in the forest; warm enclosed mill lighting; clear distant views on the ridge. Sun haze and a restrained display finish are welcome. Avoid permanent heavy blur, excessive bloom and camera movement that hides steering information.
- Life: flags move in the wind, spectators react, water shimmers, boats bob, mills turn, butterflies and birds travel, and leaves or blossom drift. Animate small shared groups or GPU geometry; static detail stays batched. World animation follows the shared simulation clock and stops with pause.
- Road readability: the racing surface remains the clearest shape. Keep curves, exits, boost panels and obstacle warnings visible from chase-camera height. Decorative scenery outside the physical course must never resemble a usable route. Any alternate route must have a visible entrance, a continuous driveable surface and a clear rejoin.

## Camera and presentation
Use a richer sky with horizon haze, a subtle sun halo and moving clouds. Add restrained speed streaks, smooth banking and ground / air response while retaining lookahead and smooth turbo widening. Modulate atmosphere by physical sector position with smooth transitions. Make impact and boost effects feel energetic without covering the road. Preserve widescreen and touch layouts.

## Four different racing experiences

- **Windmill Wilds:** a warm country-fair road trip. Combine festive open meadow, enveloping woodland, a dramatic hilltop reveal, textured timber crossing, genuinely working mill machinery and blossom-filled orchards. Living countryside, wind and moving water connect the lap. Add a forest or meadow off-road choice distinct from the orchard boost cut.
- **Neon Harbor:** a bustling city after dark. Contrast wide reflective waterfront, dense market stalls, an industrial loading hall, an elevated skyline quay, animated port machinery and a boulevard of layered neon architecture. Traffic light sequences, ferry silhouettes, scrolling displays and steam give urban life. A service lane should look and drive differently from the promenade; avoid another countryside road with bright buildings alongside it.
- **Sunstone Ruins:** an expedition through an ancient landscape. Sculpt dunes and stratified canyon walls, create oasis vegetation and animated water, reveal monumental ruins at the crest, then compress into a richly carved temple and reopen to a pillared courtyard. Dust, cloth canopies, torchlight and mechanical ancient motifs create life. Sandy cuts and a temple processional line should demand different choices from urban or alpine routes.
- **Frostpeak Festival:** a playful mountain resort. Detailed glowing chalets, snow-laden woodland, dramatic serrated peaks, a working ski lift, summit structures, a fast banked descent, a reflective ice section and animated finish crowds. Wind-blown snow and restrained flakes support altitude and temperature. A powder shoulder or alternate alpine line contrasts with the grip change on ice and the main slalom.

The existing proposals in `windmill-wilds.md` and `proposals/` establish useful landmarks and pacing. Use them as a reference while pushing course personality and richness much further. User authorization allows changes to layouts, surfaces and alternate routes beyond the older proposal's minimal scope. Keep reliable ground, understandable warning cues and fair passing space.

## Implementation contract for five agents

Exactly one agent owns each complete course, plus one global graphics agent. The four course agents own their descriptor and world module under `src/courses/<id>*.js`; the Windmill agent also owns `src/rendering/course-world.js`, its existing specialized world implementation. Each may update its proposal and relevant course tests for substantive route changes. Global graphics owns `src/game.js`, `src/rendering/graphics.js`, `src/rendering/textures.js`, `src/rendering/visuals.js` and new global effect modules. The integrating agent owns shared track construction, course runtime, placement helpers, overall documentation and integration validation. Do not edit another agent's files.

Use the existing `src/courses/CONTRACT.md` API and coordinate extensions centrally. Static objects remain batchable, animated objects must be declared in the world's `animated` collection, all geometry must remain finite, and the scene should assemble without browser-only dependencies in course rendering tests. Use seeded original assets and the already bundled licensed asset library; locally bundle and credit any new downloaded assets. Keep course text signs omitted as requested by the existing project design; use material changes, geometry, flags, lights and sightlines to communicate paths.

Shared track support will accept optional `verges` entries: `{section,startFraction,endFraction,side,extraWidth,material,grip,drag}`. Side is -1 or +1, widths are metres; entries provide a smoothly opening driveable off-road shoulder with the same progress and ground query as the main line. Rendering and collisions consume the same data. Materials may use grass, sand, snow, gravel, paving, needles or wood. Main-route surface overrides already use `surfaces`. These enable additional continuous route choices without inventing a disconnected decorative path. Keep new broad scenery footprints outside every physical boundary. Request any other shared extension from the integrator.

## Performance and delivery
Accept a modest cost for better graphics, while keeping static geometry batched, textures shared, animated groups bounded, and adaptive pixel density. Prefer 512–1024px reusable textures over oversized unique maps. Avoid hundreds of transparent overlapping meshes, unnecessary shadow casters and a separate dynamic light per prop. Keep the game self-contained. If using downloaded assets, bundle them locally and record source and license; do not depend on runtime external requests.

Validate every sector from the chase camera, both route choices, transitions, the mill portal, pause and restart. Verify AI can still complete three laps, projection / checkpoints cannot skip the course, shells respect actual boundaries, and jumps stay bounded. Record browser render counters and distinguish software-browser measurements from real-device performance. The acceptance standard is four visibly richer, more alive courses, each with a distinct identity, changing experiences, and useful driving choices.
